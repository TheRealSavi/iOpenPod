Manage your iPod without iTunes

iOpenPod lets you add music to your iPod, edit track information, and organize playlists. It’s free and open source, for Windows, macOS, and Linux.

It also handles podcasts, photos, Genius Mixes, movies, and subtitles. FLAC audio and MP4 video are converted as needed during sync, so you don’t have to convert your files in another app.

Why iOpenPod

I’ve spent two years reverse engineering iPods, researching their formats, and testing how different models behave. I care about these devices and want them to be easy to use.

iOpenPod is an ongoing project with extensive testing behind it. It wasn’t blindly generated in a week. The code, research, and development history are open for anyone to inspect.

My aim is to make iOpenPod the only third-party sync tool you need, and one you can trust with your library.

John Gibbons, iOpenPod developer

Convert audio during sync

Convert FLAC and other supported formats to audio your iPod can play. Choose the encoder and bitrate in Settings.

Conversion requires FFmpeg and FFprobe, installed separately. Optional Chromaprint matching can recognize the same recording in different formats. About FLAC conversion.

Moving from iTunes or another manager

iOpenPod can read the library already on a supported iPod. Connecting it and browsing your music does not require replacing that library.

Before making changes

Create a Backup Snapshot, then choose the media you want to add. Library edits save automatically by default; turn on Draft all changes to review those edits first. Sync has its own review step.

No libgpod dependency

iOpenPod uses its own iPodDB engine to read and write iPod databases. It does not require libgpod. Testing on individual iPod models is ongoing.

Free and open source

iOpenPod is free software for Windows, macOS, and Linux under GPL-3.0-or-later. Get a native download, install from PyPI with uv, or install the Windows app from Microsoft Store. Mac App Store, Flathub, and Snap Store remain planned.

Other managers’ settings

Use the media folders on your computer as the source for syncing. Direct import of another manager’s database or settings is not promised.
